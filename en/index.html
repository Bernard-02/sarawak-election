    Voting for the first time

    For first-time voters and anyone unfamiliar with Sarawak elections. First understand what this election decides, then see what you need to do.

    What this election decides

    This is a state election, not a general election

    You are electing the 82 members of the Sarawak State Legislative Assembly (DUN), and the result decides who forms the state government. The general election, which chooses Members of Parliament and decides the Prime Minister, is a separate election and is not part of this one.

    You vote only in your own constituency

    Sarawak is divided into 82 state constituencies, and each elects one assembly member. You get one ballot paper and choose one candidate; the candidate with the most votes in the constituency wins.

    Who governs

    The party or coalition that wins a majority (at least 42 seats) forms the state government, and its leader becomes Premier.

    What is different this time

    Voting at 18 and automatic voter registration took effect on 15 December 2021, too late for the last state election three days later. So this is the first Sarawak state election in which 18- to 20-year-olds can vote. The electoral roll has grown from about 1.25 million at the last election to 2.059 million.

    Do I need to register first?

    You do not need to apply. Citizens aged 18 and over are registered as voters automatically. But you should confirm two things: that your name is on the electoral roll for this election, and which constituency you are registered in. For both, the result shown by the EC's MySPR Semak is what counts.

    What happens on polling day

    1. Bring your identity card to the polling centreGo to the polling centre where you are registered and find your voting stream (saluran).
    2. Identity checkHand your identity card to the first clerk and show both hands for inspection. The clerk will read out your name and identity card number; listen to make sure they are yours.
    3. Indelible inkA clerk will ask you to dip a finger in indelible election ink, which shows you have been issued a ballot paper.
    4. Collect your ballot paperA clerk will hand you one ballot paper.
    5. Mark an XAt the voting booth, use a pen to mark an “X” in the blank space beside the name and symbol of the candidate you choose. Choose one only.
    6. Put it in the ballot boxFold the ballot paper and drop it in the ballot box. That is all.

    Polling centre hours

    Opening hours are not the same at every polling centre, so none are listed here. Before you set off, use the EC's MySPR Semak to check the hours for your centre.

    On the day

    • You must bring your identity card.
    • Mobile phones may not be used in the voting stream; if you bring one, leave it in the place provided.
    • Do not wear or carry clothing, caps or face masks showing the name or symbol of a party or candidate, or you may be refused entry to the polling centre.

    The last two points come from reminders the EC issued for other recent elections. For this election, the rules are those the EC announces nearer the time.

    Common terms

    DUN
    The State Legislative Assembly. Its 82 members, also called ADUN, are who you are electing this time.
    N.09, P.195
    Numbers beginning with N are state constituencies; those beginning with P are parliamentary constituencies. Only the N numbers matter this time.
    GPS, PH
    Coalitions, each made up of several parties. Gabungan Parti Sarawak (GPS) currently governs the state; Pakatan Harapan (PH) is in opposition in Sarawak.
    Premier
    The head of the Sarawak state government. Not the same as Malaysia's Prime Minister.
    Nomination day
    The day candidates formally file their nominations. Only then is it certain who is standing in each constituency.
    Majority
    The number of votes by which the winner beat the runner-up. The smaller the number, the closer the last contest was.
    MA63
    The Malaysia Agreement 1963. Sarawak often cites it when pressing for autonomy and rights over its resources.
    NCR
    Native customary rights land: land held by native communities under their customs.

    Pledge check

    GPS's 2021 state election manifesto made 34 pledges; the Premier said at the time they would be delivered within five years. This page checks each one against the public record to see how far it has got.

    Read the limits first
    • All 34 pledges are broad headings. This compilation obtained only the headings, not the detailed items in the manifesto booklet, so each is checked against the public figures and reports that could be found.
    • Each rating is the compiler's judgement, not an official score. Sources are attached beside each item; please check them yourself.
    • Only the governing GPS is checked, because only a governing party has the power to carry out its manifesto. Opposition manifestos never had the chance to be implemented and cannot be measured with the same yardstick.
    • Health, defence and citizenship are largely federal matters, so there is a limit to what the state government can do. Those items are marked.
    What a civil society survey found

    A public survey released in December 2024 by the civil society group Rise of Social Efforts (ROSE) found 9 of the 34 pledges rated dissatisfied, 3 of them very dissatisfied: no. 4 (customary land), no. 14 (telecommunications) and no. 15 (rural economy). ROSE stressed that the findings should not be taken as a final verdict.

    Overseas voting

    If you are overseas there are two routes: apply for a postal vote, or return to your own polling centre and vote in person on polling day.

    Overseas postal voting (Category 1B)Closes 20 Nov

    Applications are open from 26 October to 20 November (Friday). The EC's page does not say what time applications close that day, so it is best not to leave it to the last minute.

    1. Register an account on the EC's MySPR portal.
    2. Log in and apply for a postal vote, choosing Category 1B (overseas).
    3. The EC's application page does not explain how the ballot paper is sent to you or how to return it. Watch for the EC's notification, or ask the postal voting unit directly: upup@spr.gov.my / +603 8892 7080.
    Apply on MySPR The EC's official notice

    Eligibility and details are as announced by the EC.
